How Aviator Works: Step‑Based Risk and Cashout
Each aviator game round has a short pause before takeoff where you can set your stake. During this pre-round phase, you may usually place one or two independent bets, giving more flexibility in managing risk. Once the round begins, the multiplier starts at x1.00 and rises until the plane suddenly flies away. Your aim is to cash out at a moment you feel comfortable with the risk.
Numbered “how to play” steps:
Wait for any current round to finish and for the short pause to begin.
Choose your stake size within the casino’s minimum and maximum bet limits.
Optionally place a second, separate stake to use a different cashout target.
When the plane takes off, watch the rising multiplier and decide when to cash out.
If you cash out before the plane disappears, your payout equals stake multiplied by the current multiplier.
If the plane flies away before you cash out, your stake for that bet is lost.
The round flow is always the same: stake selection, takeoff, rising multiplier, then crash. The unpredictability of the crash point is what creates both the excitement and the risk. With a claimed maximum multiplier of up to x1,000,000 in rare cases, there is significant upside, but those high outcomes are extremely uncommon.

Integrity and Provably Fair System
Aviator uses a provably fair system so that round outcomes can be checked after the fact. The game combines cryptographic seeds to generate a final hash, which then determines the crash point. This process makes it possible to verify that a completed round was not altered. The exact verification interface can look slightly different depending on the casino’s implementation.
The general algorithm works in several stages:
The server creates a cryptographic seed that will be used for randomness.
This server seed is committed or encrypted before a round starts.
A client-side seed can also be added, depending on the platform.
The seeds are combined to produce a final hash value.
That hash is converted into the crash multiplier for the round.
After the round, players can compare published seeds and hashes with the rules.
This provably fair technology is a transparency tool, not a prediction engine. It confirms that past rounds were fair, but it cannot reveal or influence future crash points. Any tool, app, or “signal” claiming to predict Aviator results is unreliable and should be treated as a potential scam.
